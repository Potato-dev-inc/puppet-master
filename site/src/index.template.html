One team

One cockpit for every agent

Each agent runs in its own pane, in its real terminal. The sidebar groups them as Needs you, Working and Idle. Every pane has tabs for Terminal, Changes, Transcript, Receipt and History, and a question from the agent shows up as a card above the terminal.

Your agents, found on your PC

{{NAME}} finds the agent CLIs you have installed, shows their versions and tells you what to do about the rest. They run unmodified, in real terminals, signed in the way their vendor intended. Sign in opens a window, and {{NAME}} never sees your password.

Add any other CLI agent with a small TOML file.

Approvals in plain words

A card says what the agent wants, how risky it is and what it touches. Allow once, or Deny.

Trust a folder once, on purpose

A new folder asks for trust. You see a card and you decide. Nothing in {{NAME}} accepts a trust or permission prompt for you.

One Inbox for everything that needs you

Permission asks, questions with answer buttons, a usage limit and an agent that stopped all land in one list. Each card says what the agent wants and how risky it is. J and K move, 1 and 2 answer.

Review in a separate copy, then merge

Agents can work in their own copy of your project, so your files don't change while they try things. Changes shows the diff. Merge asks first, and if anything conflicts, nothing is merged. Second opinion asks another agent to review the work. It can only read. Each project can say how a copy gets ready: a setup command and files to copy, which are never committed.

Receipts that say what they don't know

Every turn ends in a receipt: the result, the agent, whether your checks ran. "Finished, not verified" is a real answer, and the row below it says why. When you declare checks, the receipt says whether they passed.

The same agents on your phone

Needs you, then Working, then Idle, with live terminals one tap away. It's a web app you pair once and reach over your own tunnel.

Every turn is a checkpoint

History keeps a checkpoint before and after each turn. Restore puts your files back. It doesn't change what the agent remembers. A folder that is too big switches undo off for itself, and says so.

Add {{NAME}} to your agent as an MCP server. It can start a worker in any agent, wait for it, steer it, and read back a receipt that separates "the agent finished" from "the checks passed".

Budgets, so nothing runs away

Set how many turns or minutes an agent may use. When the budget runs out it pauses after its current turn, a card lands in the Inbox, and you add 10 turns, add 30 minutes or stop. Nothing is stopped mid-way.

One memory every agent reads

Every agent in a project reads one shared memory file. {{NAME}} puts it into the files each agent already reads, and only ever changes the block between its own markers. Turn it off and it is removed.

[[dots:3::1,3,4,5,7]]Security and privacy

Your code stays on your PC. Here is what moves.

Three places data could go, and what we do about each.

Local-first

  • No cloud execution. Agents run in terminals on your machine.
  • No proxy, no pooling. {{NAME}} doesn't sit in your model traffic, share logins or rotate accounts. Agents talk to their own vendors directly.
  • Account folders stay yours. {{NAME}} points an agent at a folder you control and never reads what is inside.
  • A log you can read. Who allowed what, and when. Nothing in it can be changed from Settings.

Your tunnel

  • Three ways in. A quick Cloudflare tunnel started from your PC, your own named tunnel, or your own domain. Our servers aren't in the path.
  • Pair with a QR code. Pairing codes expire.
  • Four switches per phone. Watch, answer, use the terminal, start tasks. A phone with only Watch can't type into anything.
  • Phones expire. A phone unused for 30 days signs out. Revoke one or all at any time.

Encrypted team relay

  • Sealed before it leaves your PC. Team messages are encrypted with XChaCha20-Poly1305 and signed by the sending device with Ed25519.
  • Keys stay in Windows Credential Manager. Not in a file, a log or a message.
  • The relay sees who sends when. And sizes. Never names, branches or code. A test searches the relay's whole database for them.
  • Removal is enforced. Remove a member and the relay cuts their open connection and the team key is replaced. The admin's app also replaces it every 30 days.
  • A teammate's message is only a card. Nothing runs when it arrives.

Isolated, not sandboxed

A separate copy keeps an agent's files apart from yours. It does not confine a process. An agent still runs with your permissions, under its own permission mode. We say "isolated" and we don't say "sandboxed", because it isn't one.

Telemetry[[dots:3::0,2,4,6,8]]Coming

This page has no analytics. Usage counts in the app are not built yet. When they are: off by default, asked once, counted on your PC and sent once a week with no ID, with the exact payload shown before and after. Never prompts, code, file names, paths or errors.

[[dots:3::0,2,4,6,8]]FAQ

Questions people ask first.

How is this different from Claude Code's or Codex's own apps?

Each vendor's app is built for its own agent. {{NAME}} sits between them: one inbox for several agents, on Windows, in their real terminals, with a phone that connects over your own tunnel instead of a vendor's relay, and receipts that say what was and wasn't verified.

If you only use one agent and you're happy in its app, you may not need us. We don't run cloud tasks, and we don't try to out-polish a vendor at its own agent.

Does it use my own subscriptions? Does it proxy or pool my logins?

Your own, and no. {{NAME}} runs the unmodified agent CLI in a terminal, and you sign in the way the vendor intends. It doesn't read, copy or pool the credentials inside an account folder, and it doesn't rotate accounts to get around limits.

Which agents work today?

We test live with Claude Code and Codex. OpenCode and Kilo Code also run, and any other CLI agent can be added with a TOML file. Cursor Agent is detected but we haven't finished proving it, so it isn't on the supported list.

Is it sandboxed?

No, it's isolated. A separate copy of your project keeps an agent's files apart from yours, every turn is a checkpoint, and approvals are in plain words. But an agent still runs with your permissions, under its own permission mode.

What happens when an agent hits its usage limit?

Today you see the limit in the pane and the Inbox, and you can start the same task in another agent yourself. Handing the task over automatically, from a written brief, is coming. It isn't in the app yet.

Do I need a phone, or a team?

No to both. The phone and Co-work are optional. The desktop app works on its own.

What can undo do, and what can't it?

Restore puts your files back to the checkpoint before or after any turn. It doesn't change what the agent remembers. If a folder is too big to checkpoint, undo switches itself off for that folder and tells you.

What does it send about me?

This page has no analytics. The app has no usage counts yet; when we add them they will be off by default, with the exact payload visible before and after it's sent. Your prompts and code never go through us. Team messages are end-to-end encrypted, and the relay sees only who sends when.

Why Windows first?

It's the platform most other agent managers skip, and it's where {{NAME}} is built and tested, on Windows' own pseudo-terminals. A macOS build exists but gets less testing.

What will it cost?

Free for everything that runs on your PC. Team ($8 per active member a month) and Business ($16) are planned for the hosted services, and they aren't for sale yet. Join the waitlist and we'll tell you when they open.
